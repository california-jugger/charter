# California Jugger Alliance Charter
This document describes the purpose and structure of the California Jugger Alliance, hereafter referred to as CAJA.

## MISSION STATEMENT
CAJA exists to support the growth and development of sport jugger in the state of California. Specific objectives/goals, in no particular order:

 - Serve as a discussion forum for all teams in California on any and all jugger-related matters.  
 - Facilitate tournaments and game days within California.  
 - Support the growth of new teams in California through sharing of information, material resources, and personnel.  
 - Arbitrate disputes between California teams and within the body of CAJA as a whole, through the Wellness Committee.
 - Perform outreach and serve as liaison with the general public and external parties, including other jugger teams or organizations outside California.

Things beyond CAJA purview:

 - Wasteland jugger.  

Longer-term objectives:

 - Obtain legal status as an organization, to more easily obtain sponsorship, insurance, funding, etc.  
 - Collect and manage funds/materials for seeding new teams.

## ORGANIZATION STRUCTURE
CAJA comprises all teams within California who voluntarily participate in the organization. The official functions of CAJA are performed by three bodies: the **voting council**, **wellness committee**, and **executive committee**.

**Voting Council:** One member per active member team who votes on major matters. Teams with a vote choose their own representatives.  
  - Application onto the voting council is listed in the [Voting Rights](#voting-rights) section.
  - Any member of the council can propose a vote. Not all CAJA proceedings or activities require a vote, but if a council member demands a vote on something then majority approval (simple majority, i.e. \>50%) is required for the motion to proceed.

**Wellness Committee:** A group of volunteers, appointed by the community, who have the ability to enforce and arbitrate interpersonal disputes and relations both within the organization, and within member teams of the organization. 
 - Any CAJA community member is eligible to serve on the committee, even if they're not on an active team.
 - Wellness committee appointees require approval by majority wellness committee volunteer vote.
 - The capabilities and actions the wellness committee is permitted to take are detailed in the CAJA Grievance Procediure.
 - The work the wellness committee does should not be influenced or determined by Executive Committee or Voting Council. The wellness committee has the final say in enforcing the work it does.

**Executive Committee:** Appointed members of the community, led by the CAJA Chairperson, who execute the primary functions of NJA. Positions and responsibilities are described in more detail below.  
  - Any CAJA community member is eligible to serve on the committee, even if they’re not on an active team.  
  - Committee appointees can be nominated by themselves or any other community member. All appointees require approval by majority council vote.  
  - Committee positions are held for 1 year or until resignation or removal by council vote, whatever comes first. There are presently no term limits.  
  - Committee members may not simultaneously be on the voting council.
  - Committee members hold two central responsibilities; attend relevant meetings and complete work assigned to them by an agreed upon deadline.

### EXECUTIVE COMMITTEE ACCOUNTABILITY
To keep members of Executive Committee accountable to their position, the following protocol details the central processes through which a member of Executive Committee can be removed from the organization, besides via council vote. These processes have no interactions with each other and are kept seperate from each other. The accountability practice for the Chairperson is detailed in the impeachment section of the charter.

**VIA MEETINGS**: If a Committee member fails to communicate an inablity to attend a properly scheduled CAJA meeting (a meeting announced at least 7 days before the date of the meeting) within 3 days of the meeting, they are liable to have a meeting strike added to their record, at the discretion of the Chairperson. A Committee member is subject to removal should they receive three meeting strikes.

**VIA DEADLINES**: If a member of Executive Committee agrees, alongside someone in a position to delegate work to the Committee Member (typically the Chairperson), to complete an article of work by a specific deadline, they must ensure one of the following occurs within FIVE DAYS following the deadline;
 - The work is accomplished by the allocated deadline by the Committee member.
 - The Committee member communicates a request for an extension or cancellation of the work allocated.

Should neither of these be done, and there be no communication on the inability to complete the work or accomplish an extension, the committee member is liable to have a deadline strike added to their record. A Committee member is subject to removal should they receive two deadline strikes.


### EXECUTIVE COMMITTEE POSITIONS
Specific roles and responsibilities of the executive committee positions are described below. While certain committee members may have assistants, the official committee always comprises only five. This is to avoid organizational bloat and diffusion of accountability. New positions may be added as the organization grows and evolves, as deemed necessary by the voting council.

**Chairperson:** Administrative head of CAJA.

 - Accountability: Primary liaison between executive committee and the voting council. Subservient to the council, and responsible for making sure other committee members fulfill their roles.  
 - Public communications: Spokesperson and point person for CAJA to jugger community in CA and beyond.  
 - CAJA meetings: Schedule, plan, and moderate regularly scheduled CAJA meetings. Coordinate additional meetings with the executive committee as needed.  
 - CAJA management: Delegate tasks to committee members as appropriate, and support them in these tasks.

**SECRETARY:** Administrative assistant to the Chairperson.

 - Accountability: Communicate with and be accountable to the Chairperson. This includes attending meetings when asked.  
 - CAJA meetings: Assist the Chairperson with planning CAJA meetings. This includes scheduling and making an agenda.  
 - Agenda management: Solicit teams and general community for agenda items, and publicize agendas prior to meetings in a timely fashion.  
 - Meeting minutes: Record minutes during CAJA meetings and present them in a clean and readable fashion to the general community afterwards to uphold organizational transparency.  
 - Records keeping: Establish and maintain records of CAJA proceedings for future reference, as well as other important organizational documentation (e.g. a centralized Google Drive).

**CHIEF EVENT COORDINATOR:** Game day and tournament-related tasks.

 - Accountability: Communicate with and be accountable to the Chairperson. This includes attending meetings when asked.  
 - CAJA Events: Organize CAJA events, especially game days. This includes date/time/location logistics, hour-by-hour scheduling, competitive formats (round robin, brackets, individual events, etc.), event announcements, etc.  
 - Local tournaments: Support CAJA member teams if they organize their own events, e.g. Fog of War hosted by Ursae Majoris. This might entail enlisting help from non-hosting teams, as well as compiling general tournament resources, e.g. lists of local parks, contact numbers for insurance and field reservations, systems for round-robin/bracket assignment, etc.  
 - Out-of-state tournaments: Help organize traveling teams of CAJA players in the event member teams don’t sort their own rosters.  
 - Rulebook management: Keep and update the rulebook to be used by CAJA member teams. Major rulebook changes and decisions will be decided by the voting council with community input.  
 - Assistants: May gather assistant coordinators to help with the above responsibilities (subject to council approval).

**CHIEF OF ADVERTISING/OUTREACH:** Community growth and outreach.

 - Accountability: Communicate with and be accountable to the Chairperson. This includes attending meetings when asked.  
 - Advertising: Use social media and other means to broaden public exposure to jugger, enable easy access to the community, and direct interested people to the teams in their areas.  
 - Outreach: Coordinate with other interest groups (e.g. LARP, HEMA, fencing, kendo, etc.) as needed. This may come at the request of the Chairperson, council, community members, etc  
 - Account management: Manage existing accounts and create new ones on different platforms as needed to fulfill the general goals of this position.  
 - Assistants: May gather advertising/outreach assistants to help with the above responsibilities (subject to council approval).

**WEBMASTER:** Online community management.

 - Accountability: Communicate with and be accountable to the Chairperson. This includes attending meetings when asked.  
 - Online community moderator: Set rules for CAJA Discord and maintain online community health/standards/discipline.  
 - Server transition: Facilitate transition from current Ursae Majoris Discord server to a CAJA server. Ursae Majoris has plans to make a separate Discord server for team-internal affairs, which is  - yond the scope of this position.  
 - Assistants: May gather assistant moderators to help with the above responsibilities, (subject to council approval).

**CHIEF WELLNESS COORDINATOR:** Wellness committee manager and wellbeing support.

 - Accountability: Communicate with and be accountable to the Chairperson. This includes attending meetings when asked.
 - Wellness committee management: The chief wellness coordinator will always be a volunteer in the wellness committee, but has no larger say in any specific wellness case than any other given volunteer. The coordinator is responsible for overseeing management and maitenance of the health of the wellness committee, and is the primary contact in relation to the larger committee as a whole.

The listed responsibilities are intended to give an idea of what each position might look like in an idealized scenario. The specifics will shift and evolve with time and individual tenures. Right now, the only non-negotiable facet is accountability, whether to the council or CAJA Chairperson. In the end we’re all volunteers who care about jugger and our community, so there should be little we can’t sort out through good-faith discussion.



## CAJA MEETINGS
All major decisions within CAJA require approval of the voting council via a live vote. The voting council and executive committee are expected to convene at least once every few months in a meeting, and members of all CAJA-affiliated teams may attend. Executive Committee may meet without the presence of voting council to delegate work and get progress reports should they choose to.
Any meeting that involves more than four people must be clearly announced and scheduled to relevant parties 7 days before the meeting for the relevant parties to be held accountable for attending the meeting. Should the meeting be announced sooner than 7 days before the date of the meeting, no member of Executive Committee can be held accountable per the Executive Committee Accountability procedures for not attending the meeting. 

An ideal meeting is structured approximately as follows:

1. **Prior:** Voting council or executive committee members submit topics for discussion to the Secretary. The Secretary posts a meeting agenda to the CAJA Discord server, ideally a few days before the scheduled meeting.  
2. **Day of:** The Chairperson (or pre-appointed substitute) initiates the meeting and takes a quorum. Not all council or committee members are required to attend every meeting, but major decisions can only be made with council majority approval during a vote with sufficient voting members present. The Chairperson moderates the meeting per the agenda, prioritizing topics at their discretion. The Secretary takes minutes and notes on discussions and votes.  
3. **After:** The Secretary submits minutes to the council in the CAJA Discord server, and optionally also to public channels for all California members. The Chairperson and Secretary schedule the next CAJA meeting with input from those expected to be present.

## VOTING RIGHTS

Voting council is composed as group of one representative from each club under CAJA, alongside a miscellaneous vote for players who do not feel represented by any given club.

CAJA defines a club as the following;
1. A club is a group of organized jugger players that practice together in a distinct geographical location.
2. A club must run at least two practices each quarter that are directly overseen by the clubs leadership - attending a practice hosted by a seperate club does not count to this.
   a. A practice is defined by CAJA an event hosted by the club with the intent of playing and facilitating the sport of jugger, with at least 5 people present over the course of the practice.
3. A club has at least five active players.
   
Each club is given one vote, and there is always a miscellaneous vote. Votes do not scale with club size.
A club may apply for voting rights via the following application method;

1. A member of club leadership (hereby referred to as the officer) should make a post in the "voting-issues" forum channel in the California Jugger discord titled "(Team Name) VOTING COUNCIL APPLICATION"
2. Within the application, the officer should list;
    a. The name of the club.
    b. The geographical location of the club.
    c. The current active number of players.
    d. The location of practices, and when they are run.
    e. The distinction of the club from other nearby jugger clubs, if necessary.
3. The officer should then provide at least 2 forms of photo or video evidence of a practice.

The legitimacy of the application is down to the discretion of the voting council. The clubs entrance into the voting council will be voted upon at the next general meeting *at least fourteen days* after the initial request is made.

One miscellaneous vote is to be provided. The miscellaneous vote must have at least 2 players represented under the vote to be active. The vote aims to capture the thoughts of players who *do not feel represented under any club vote in voting council* and *are not connected to enough players in their area to form a new club*. 
The miscellaneous vote should be covered by a representative who feels confident that they can gather the interests and opinions of the players captured under the vote.

A player may sign up for the miscellaneous vote group via the following application method;

1. The player should make a post in the "voting-issues" forum channel in the California Jugger discord titled "(Player First Name / Discord handle) MISCELLANOUS VOTE APPLICATION"
2. Within the application, the player should list;
    a. Their name and time played.
    b. Their geographical location.
    c. An explanation for why they do not feel represented by any club currently on voting council.
    d. An explanation on why seeking club voting status is infeasible from their position.

The legitimacy of the application is down to the discretion of the voting council. The players entrance into the miscellaneous vote will be voted upon at the next general meeting *at least fourteen days* after the initial request is made.

Voting rights are to be reexamined at the end of the first quarter and the third quarter of the year. This examination is to ensure that every club in the voting council is still active and thoroughly represented.
To keep track of activeness, the Secretary is to keep a log of every general meeting attended by each member of voting council, and each miscellaneous voter. If any individual voting body has missed *3 or more general meetings* since the previous reexamination, it is to be flagged at the next meeting.

The reexamination process falls as follows;
1. The President organizes and announces, at a general meeting either at the end of the 1st/3rd quarter or beginning of the 2nd/4th quarter, wherein a role-call of voting council is to be expected.
2. Each member of voting council, and voter under the miscellaneous vote is to attend. The president is to check with each member of voting council that they are interested in renewing their position on voting council.
3. Any voting body flagged for inactivity is to be warned. If any voting body recieves a warning at two consecutive reexaminations, they are to be removed from voting council for the next complete quarter. They may reapply to voting council from the beginning of the following quarter. Voting bodies with only one warning should be reminded of this fact.
4. Each member that concurs to continue remaining on voting council is to have their voting rights renewed until the next reexamination.
5. If a member cannot send a representative to the reexamination meeting, they are to be sent a message by the Secretary either before or after the meeting informing them of their number of missed general meetings and asking of their intent to remain on voting council. If the member of voting council does not reply within 24 hours of the beginning of the reexamination meeting and no other representative is accessible, they are to be removed for the next complete quarter.

## EDITING THE CHARTER
This charter is expected to change as CAJA grows. The charter as available on the `main` branch (available at [github.com/california-jugger/charter](https://github.com/california-jugger/charter)) is always the current active charter.  
The process for making edits is as follows:
1. Anyone makes changes to this document on a fork of this repository.
2. When they are satisfied with the changes, they create a pull request on github and publicize this to (at least) the members of the voting council. The pull request must include, at a minimum:
     - A brief summary of the changes made
     - Why the changes are believed to be necessary
3. Anyone may discuss the changes and provide feedback or suggestions in the comments of the pull request.
4. At the next CAJA general meeting, the voting council votes to determine the status of the pull request. They must choose to
     - Close the pull request (reject the changes)
     - Merge the pull request (accept the changes)
     - Abstain
   Both actions - close or merge - require a 2/3 majority to pass. If this does not happen, the pull request remains open for comments and updates, and the vote is deferred until the next general meeting.
